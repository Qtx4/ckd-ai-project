Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is a long-term condition in which the kidneys gradually lose their ability to filter waste, toxins, and excess fluids from the blood.

The main causes of CKD are:
- Diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar)
- Hypertension (high blood pressure)
- Kidney infections
- Genetic disorders
- Long-term use of certain medicines (like painkillers)

---

CKD STAGES BASED ON eGFR:

Stage 1: eGFR ≥ 90 (Normal kidney function but some damage present)
Stage 2: eGFR 60–89 (Mild kidney damage)
Stage 3a: eGFR 45–59 (Mild to moderate loss of function)
Stage 3b: eGFR 30–44 (Moderate to severe loss)
Stage 4: eGFR 15–29 (Severe kidney damage)
Stage 5: eGFR < 15 (Kidney failure, dialysis required)

---

COMMON SYMPTOMS OF CKD:
- Fatigue and weakness
- Swelling in legs, ankles, or face
- Nausea and vomiting
- Loss of appetite
- Foamy urine (protein leakage)
- Frequent urination at night

---

DIET AND LIFESTYLE IN CKD:
- Reduce salt (sodium) intake
- Control protein intake in advanced stages
- Avoid junk food and processed food
- Control blood sugar and blood pressure
- Drink water as advised by doctor
- Avoid NSAID painkillers like ibuprofen without prescription
- Regular exercise is important

---

TREATMENT OPTIONS:
- Medicines to control blood pressure and sugar
- Dietary management
- Dialysis in stage 5
- Kidney transplant in end-stage kidney disease

---

IMPORTANT INFORMATION:
- CKD cannot be completely cured, but its progression can be slowed.
- Early detection helps prevent kidney failure.
- Regular monitoring of eGFR and creatinine is important.

---

Q&A:

Q: What is eGFR?
A: eGFR (Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate) measures kidney function.

Q: Can CKD be reversed?
A: No, CKD cannot be fully reversed, but progression can be slowed.

Q: When is dialysis needed?
A: Dialysis is needed in Stage 5 CKD when kidneys stop functioning properly.

Q: What is the main cause of CKD?
A: Diabetes and high blood pressure are the most common causes.
